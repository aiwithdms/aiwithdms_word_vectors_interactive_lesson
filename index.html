From Words to Vectors to AI Meaning

Computers work with numbers. Word embeddings help AI represent words as numbers so it can compare patterns, relationships and meaning.

King−Man+Woman≈Queen
KING→MAN+WOMAN→QUEEN

1. What is a vector?

Think of a vector as a list of numbers, or a location on a map.

AA point on a map

On a simple map, [3, 2] means move 3 along X and 2 along Y. Those coordinates locate a point.

X →↑ Y
[3, 2]

Two numbers describe a position.

BWords become numbers

A word embedding model turns a word into a numerical vector that reflects patterns learned from how words are used.

Word → Embedding model → Vector
The numbers are learned from text; they are not manually assigned definitions.

CMany dimensions

Our map has only 2 dimensions. Real word embeddings often use hundreds or thousands of numbers.

[0.12−0.480.91…

Usually, each number does not have one simple human-readable meaning.

2. The famous word-vector analogy

The model learns from language patterns that some relationships between words are similar.

Look at the relationship

In this classic example, the change from man → king is approximately like the change from woman → queen in the learned vector space.

Man→King
Woman→Queen

Vector arithmetic

Each word is replaced with its vector. The model does arithmetic on those vectors—not on the spelling of the words.

v(King)−v(Man)+v(Woman)

The result is a target point in embedding space.

Find the closest word

The system compares the target vector with vectors for words in its vocabulary. If Queen is the closest match, it returns Queen.

Important: the result is approximate. Different models can return different answers, and embeddings can reflect biases in their training text.

4. Where do embeddings help?

Semantic searchFind results by meaning, not just exact keywords.
ChatbotsHelp systems connect a question with related language patterns.
RecommendationsFind items or content that are related to a user's interests.
Text analysisSupport classification, clustering and other language tasks.

5. Remember these four ideas

✓
Vector
A list of numbers.
✓
Embedding
A model's numerical representation of a word or other input.
✓
Similarity and relationships
Vectors let a model compare learned patterns in a mathematical space.
✓
Analogy
King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen is a famous example, not a universal rule.
One-minute explanation you can say aloud:
“A vector is a list of numbers. A word embedding model turns words into vectors based on patterns in language. Once words are represented as points in a mathematical space, we can compare their relationships. That is the idea behind the classic example King minus Man plus Woman approximately gives Queen.”
